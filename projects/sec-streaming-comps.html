Real Filed Data
Real data, real source: Every figure on this page is transcribed directly from each company's audited SEC filings — 10-K exhibits and 8-K earnings releases — not estimated or simulated. pull_edgar_live.py pulls the same figures live from the SEC EDGAR XBRL API (data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/) using requests + pandas — no API key required, 10 req/sec fair-access limit. Run it locally any time a new 10-K drops to refresh the dataset. Accession numbers for every source filing are cited in build_streaming_comps.py.
Two notes on comparability: Disney's fiscal year ends in late September, so its "FY2024" spans Oct 2023–Sept 2024, not the calendar year the other three report on — shown as filed, not restated. And Paramount Global merged with Skydance Media in August 2025 to become Paramount Skydance Corp (ticker PSKY); the figures here predate that merger and are reported under the legacy Paramount Global entity, which is what existed for all three fiscal years compared.

The Case Study

Five-Step Walkthrough

1
Why Cross-Company Comps Need Real Audit Discipline
Margin and leverage analysis, applied with the same rigor as any financial audit
▾

A financial statement audit asks one question over and over: does what a company reports as profit hold up once you strip out the one-time noise — impairments, restructuring charges, discontinued operations — and look at the recurring economics underneath? A cross-company comparison is that same question asked across four companies at once, with the added discipline of making sure the comparison is apples-to-apples.

Streaming and media is a useful sector to prove this on, because 2022–2024 was three years of extremely public financial whiplash: massive content spend, a "streaming wars" subscriber land-grab, and then a wave of multi-billion-dollar goodwill impairments as the market repriced linear TV. That volatility means the numbers actually move — which is what makes a comparison worth building in the first place.

  • Revenue growth — who's actually gaining ground, and at what rate
  • Operating margin — how much of every revenue dollar survives content and distribution costs before financing and one-offs
  • Net margin — the bottom-line answer, including the impairment charges that operating margin can hide
2
The Dataset & Sourcing
Four CIKs, three fiscal years, pulled from the SEC's free XBRL API
▾

Every public company's filings are indexed under a CIK (Central Index Key). Once you have it, SEC EDGAR's XBRL API returns every structured financial fact a company has ever tagged in a filing — for free, with no API key, straight from data.sec.gov.

3
Metrics & the Pandas/NumPy Layer
Margins, YoY growth, and 2-year CAGR — vectorized, not looped
▾

Once the raw revenue / operating income / net income figures are in a tidy long-format DataFrame (one row per company-year), the derived metrics are a few vectorized NumPy operations — no manual loops over rows:

Operating Margin
op_margin = op_income / revenue
How much of each revenue dollar survives content, distribution, and SG&A costs before interest, taxes, and one-time impairments.
Net Margin
net_margin = net_income / revenue
The bottom-line number — the one that includes the goodwill impairment charges that gutted WBD and Paramount's 2024 results.
YoY Revenue Growth
groupby('company')['revenue'].pct_change()
Per-company year-over-year growth, computed with a single grouped pandas operation rather than manual prior-year lookups.
2-Year CAGR (FY22→FY24)
(end / start) ** (1/2) − 1
Smooths single-year noise out of the growth comparison — useful here since FY2023 was an unusually volatile middle year for two of the four companies.
Python
# margins — vectorized with NumPy, guarding against divide-by-zero
df['operating_margin_pct'] = np.round(
    np.divide(df['operating_income_m'], df['revenue_m']) * 100, 1
)
df['net_margin_pct'] = np.round(
    np.divide(df['net_income_m'], df['revenue_m']) * 100, 1
)

# YoY growth per company, via groupby + pct_change
df['revenue_yoy_pct'] = np.round(
    df.groupby('company')['revenue_m'].pct_change() * 100, 1
)

Matrix (company × fiscal year) with conditional formatting on net margin, a clustered column chart for revenue, and a KPI card strip — all built from streaming_comps_full.csv using the DAX measures below.
Operating Margin %
DIVIDE(SUM(comps[operating_income_m]), SUM(comps[revenue_m]))
Net Margin %
DIVIDE(SUM(comps[net_income_m]), SUM(comps[revenue_m]))
YoY Revenue Growth
VAR PY = CALCULATE(SUM(comps[revenue_m]), SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(comps[fiscal_year]))
RETURN DIVIDE(SUM(comps[revenue_m]) − PY, PY)
Margin Rank
RANKX(ALL(comps[company]), [Net Margin %], , DESC)
