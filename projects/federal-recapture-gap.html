Real data, real source: Every figure on this page traces back to PaymentAccuracy.gov — the official Treasury/OMB/DOJ site tracking federal payment integrity — as compiled and cited in the Congressional Research Service report "Improper Payments: Ongoing Challenges and Recent Legislative Proposals" (R48296, updated June 2026), with individual program figures cross-referenced against GAO audit reports. Nothing here is simulated. CRS reports are U.S. government works and not subject to copyright, so the source figures are reproduced directly rather than paraphrased through an intermediary.
One honest caveat, straight from the source: the CRS report itself flags that PaymentAccuracy.gov's recovery data is incomplete — it explains the gap between money identified for recapture and money actually recovered, but not why the other 74% of overpayments were never pursued in the first place. This dashboard reports the funnel exactly as documented, without filling that gap with a guess.

Pre-Audit Detection vs. Post-Audit Recovery, at Federal Scale
A nine-figure gap between what's found and what's actually collected
▾

Every year, agencies estimate how much they overpaid under the Payment Integrity Information Act. Some of that gets flagged for a recovery audit. A smaller amount actually comes back. The distance between "identified" and "recovered" is the same audit-efficiency question any recovery program has to answer — just denominated in tens of billions of taxpayer dollars.

  • Overpayments Identified — the pre-audit finding: what agencies estimate they paid incorrectly
  • Identified for Recapture — the post-audit decision: what's actually flagged for a recovery attempt
  • Actually Recovered — the real-world result, after collection, waivers, and write-offs

3
Audit Math — What the Funnel Actually Measures
Three different recovery rates, depending on the denominator
▾

The single most misleading move in reading government recovery data is picking the wrong denominator. There are three legitimate "recovery rate" numbers here, and they tell very different stories:

Recapture Identification Rate
$39B / $152B = 25.7%
Of everything overpaid, only about a quarter is even flagged for a recovery attempt. This is the number the CRS report says PaymentAccuracy.gov doesn't adequately explain.
Collection Efficiency
$24B / $39B = 61.5%
Of the money agencies actually pursue, they recover about 62 cents on the dollar — a genuinely respectable collection rate once the target is set.
True Recovery Rate
$24B / $152B = 15.8%
The honest, all-in number: of every dollar overpaid, about 16 cents ultimately comes back. This is the metric that matters for a "recovery program ROI" conversation, and it's the one usually left out of the headline.
