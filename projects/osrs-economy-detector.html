The Case Study

Five-Step Walkthrough

1
Why the GE Needs Forensic Monitoring
An economy with 200M+ daily transactions and no built-in anti-manipulation tooling
▾

The Old School RuneScape Grand Exchange processes hundreds of millions of gold coin transactions every day across 3,700+ tradeable items. It operates without an automated surveillance layer — Jagex moderates via player reports and periodic bot bans, but there is no statistical monitoring of individual item price behavior against historical baselines.

This creates three categories of recurring market events that a flat threshold or a simple price alert would miss entirely:

  • Coordinated manipulation: player groups buy out an item's supply to restrict volume, wait for price to spike, then dump simultaneously. The signature — price up, volume down, then price crash with volume flood — is invisible to a percentage-change alert but immediately obvious to a compound signal classifier
  • Bot-ban supply crashes: when Jagex bans a wave of farming bots, the items they farmed see their market supply vanish overnight. Dragon Bones, nature runes, and alching supplies all carry this pattern: volume drops and price spikes simultaneously, then normalizes as legitimate players fill the supply gap
  • Content-patch demand shocks: a new update makes an item BiS (Best-in-Slot) and demand spikes within hours of the patch. Without a per-item baseline, there's no way to distinguish a legitimate demand spike from artificial manipulation in real time

This is the same monitoring problem as billing anomaly detection — and the same statistical method applies.

2
The Dataset
15 items from high-value raids to stable commodity runes — pulled via OSRS Wiki API
▾

The ingame_economy_detector_osrs.py script fetches 18 months of daily price and volume history for 15 curated GE items using the OSRS Wiki Real-Time Prices API (prices.runescape.wiki/api/v1/osrs/timeseries?timestep=24h&id={itemId}). The API is a partnership between the Old School Wiki and RuneLite, tracking actual in-game transactions. No rate limit — but a descriptive User-Agent is required per their acceptable use policy.

Items span four categories deliberately chosen to produce different anomaly signatures:

Raid Rewards
Twisted Bow · Scythe of Vitur · Tumeken's Shadow
Highest-value items in the game — billion+ GP. Sensitive to CoX/ToB/ToA meta changes and endgame patch notes. Extremely low daily volume = large z-scores from small absolute changes.
Iconic Weapons
Abyssal Whip · Dragon Claws · Toxic Blowpipe
Mid-to-high tier, recognizable to most players. Sensitive to PK meta shifts, bot-ban waves hitting slayer drop rates, and spec weapon tournament demand spikes.
Armour
Bandos Chestplate · Armadyl Crossbow · Elysian Spirit Shield
GWD and Corporeal Beast drops. Price reflects bossing meta and supply from mid-tier PvM players — slower-moving but subject to long-period erosion from power-creep.
Commodity Basket
Nature Rune · Blood Rune · Prayer Potion · Dragon Bones · Shark
High-volume, price-stable supplies used to track currency inflation. When alching bot bans happen, nature rune prices spike. When prayer training bots get banned, dragon bones spike. These are the canary items.
3
Detection Methodology
Three parallel layers — price z-score, velocity z-score, commodity inflation index
▾

The same three-layer approach from the billing anomaly detection work, adapted for GE-specific patterns. Each layer runs independently; their combination per item-day determines the anomaly type.

Method 1 — Price Z-Score
z = (price − μ₁₄) / σ₁₄
flag if |z| ≥ 2.5
14-day rolling mean and std, shifted 1 day so the current day isn't in its own baseline. Std floored at 4% of rolling mean — prevents blowup on unusually flat periods.
Method 2 — Volume Z-Score
z = (volume − μ₁₄) / σ₁₄
flag if |z| ≥ 2.8
Same rolling structure. Threshold is slightly higher than price (2.8 vs 2.5) because daily volume is naturally spikier than price and produces more noise at the lower bar.
Method 3 — Inflation Index
basket_Δ₁₄ ≥ 15%
→ CURRENCY INFLATION
Five commodity items tracked as a market basket. If the basket rises 15%+ over 14 days, a gold-supply inflation event is flagged regardless of individual item signals.

The compound classifier maps signal combinations to GE-specific event types. The combination is what separates genuine market events from noise:

Python
def classify(row):
    # price up + volume restricted = artificial scarcity (manipulation)
    if z_price >= 2.5 and z_vol <= -2.0: return "PRICE MANIPULATION"

    # price crashes + volume floods = dump or bot-ban supply crash
    if z_price <= -2.5 and z_vol >= 2.5: return "DUMP / BOT BAN"

    # volume spike, price flat = wash trading (accounts trading between themselves)
    if z_vol >= 2.8 and abs(z_price) < 2.0: return "WASH TRADING"

    # single signals
    if z_price >= 2.5: return "PRICE SPIKE"   # content patch demand / content leak
    if z_price <= -2.5: return "PRICE DROP"
    if erosion_14d <= -0.22: return "SLOW EROSION"  # power-creep, new alternatives
    return "NORMAL"
